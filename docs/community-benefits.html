Terms every draft starts with

Working draft for [community], [site and phases], [landowner], [operator], [guarantor] and [resident signatories]. These are proposed terms. Fill in brackets with local evidence; nothing on this page has been agreed for your site.

1. Let residents choose who negotiates.
Before any agreement vote, residents select representatives in an open meeting. Include renters and neighbors outside the taxing boundary. Give them an independent adviser paid from developer escrow, interpretation and a public response to proposed changes. Publish the draft for [review period]. Participation does not imply consent. Keep the option to decline.
2. Price the benefit separately.
The operator pays [amount] on [milestone], then [amount and inflation adjustment] each year to [fund holder]. A resident-majority board with published conflict rules selects awards; the treasurer posts receipts and spending quarterly. Show taxes, abatements, infrastructure reimbursement and voluntary gifts on separate lines. No double counting.
3. Attach the right document.
List each obligation, payer, due date, baseline, measurement method, verifier and remedy. Identify who can enforce it. Put fund obligations in the signed agreement, land-use conditions in lawful permits, and utility cost allocation in applicable approved service terms. Local counsel checks authority and signatories. This draft does not replace any required environmental review, public hearing or Tribal consultation, or alter treaty rights.
4. Make a missed promise visible and collectible.
Residents select an independent monitor with access to invoices, meters and anonymized payroll under the signed agreement. The operator pays [annual monitoring budget] into a separate account through closure. The monitor posts a quarterly public ledger: promised, due, verified, late or disputed, with evidence. Residents can file complaints with [office] by phone or online; acknowledge in [days] and publish anonymized dispositions. Give written notice and [cure period]. Identify who may draw [security] for each unpaid obligation after cure, who decides disputes and how to appeal. A resident right to enforce must be written in.
5. Cover sale, closure and unfinished phases.
Before construction the owner posts independently costed restoration security, updated at [interval]. Require a buyer to assume duties in writing and replace security before release of the original obligor. Identify which payments survive early closure. Publish signed schedules, amendments, annual audits and the final restoration inspection.

Fill in one row for each promise

[Obligation] · [baseline and target, with units] · [responsible signatory] · [budget and payment source] · [due date] · [public evidence and independent verifier] · [notice, cure and remedy] · [next review date].

At the last meeting before signing, read back unresolved items in public. Attach the completed rows and supporting schedules to the agreement; name the people authorized to sign, the date of the vote and the custodian of the public copy.

Draft 01 · Rural county

Protect wells, working land and volunteer services

Start here if a campus would draw on a small water system or bring heavy construction to farm roads. Invite well owners, farm operators, the water supplier and the fire district. Approach affected Tribal governments separately, on their own terms.

Before excavation: document the water supply.
The operator funds an independent lab chosen by [water district and resident representatives]. Offer opt-in well depth and quality tests before work; keep household locations private. Attach peak-day and annual withdrawal limits by source, consumption, discharge quality, monitoring points and drought-stage reductions. Keep daily meter readings by source. Each month, publish the peak day and total, return flows, drought stage, exceedances and any corrective action. The independent verifier checks the underlying daily logs. At [trigger], fund temporary drinking water and an independent investigation; the agreement sets the evidence needed for permanent repairs.
Before the first truck: pay the actual cost of access.
The road superintendent and volunteer fire chief price road repairs, training and equipment. The developer deposits [costed amount] before construction. Compare haul-route and bridge inspections before and after heavy traffic. The county may draw agreed repair security for unrepaired damage after notice and cure.
At opening: invest in what stays.
Set aside [annual amount] of the separate benefits fund for resident-chosen priorities such as paid trades training or shared farm connectivity. The fund board publishes awards, who can use them and receipts each year. If training is funded, contract for [number] paid places per cohort at [wage] and publish completion and placement counts. If a payment is late, the named fund holder uses the collection schedule in the signed agreement.

Why ask? In August 2026, Salix, Iowa, voted down a proposed pause; a local organizer asked for both town and rural residents to be heard. That vote did not approve a campus. In New Mexico, Doña Ana County’s signed Project Jupiter agreement separates water commitments from scheduled payments. Its guarantee covers specified unpaid amounts; it does not guarantee damages for every breach.

Draft 02 · Growing town or suburb

Keep the town solvent if phase two never opens

Start here if a town is considering an incentive or expanding services in anticipation of future growth. Bring the school district, utility, finance director, workforce college and residents of newly annexed areas to the table.

Before an incentive vote: show who gains and who pays.
The finance director publishes a phase-by-phase comparison: taxes collected, abatements, school and fire revenue, staffing and debt service with and without the project. Districts review their own figures. Before releasing each locally controlled installment, verify [permanent jobs, wage and investment milestones]. Put proportionate recapture and appeal provisions in the incentive agreement where authorized.
Before expanding service: price a cancellation.
The operator pays [specified road and water works] against engineer-certified milestones. Ask the utility for approved service terms covering grid upgrades, deposits, minimum billing and exit liability. Model a delayed or canceled phase. The utility or its regulator handles electric charges. Before the town commits public funds, secure its nonrecoverable costs, including orders placed but not finished. Name who can draw on cancellation, update security as costs change and deduct the value of reusable works.
Every operating year: count people and payments.
Agree a minimum fund payment for each operating building, its first due date and what is owed if the next building never opens. The operator funds [number] paid places per cohort at [wage] with the college, which reports starts, completions and placements after six months. Verify construction job-years separately from permanent positions using aggregate payroll. For missed training places, require funded replacement places by [date].

Why ask? Marshalltown’s August 2026 ordinance included substantial expansions and recognized vested rights. Inventory pending applications before projecting growth. A separate Cedar Rapids amendment pairs qualifying jobs per phase with a fund of up to $18 million over 20 years. The cap is neither an annual payment nor proof of receipt.

Draft 03 · Dense urban neighborhood

Measure impacts where people live and sleep

Start here when apartments, schools or clinics share a fence line or truck route with the site. Let tenant groups and nearby blocks choose their representatives, including people who cannot attend an evening hearing.

Before permits: establish the neighborhood baseline.
The operator funds a resident-selected acoustician to measure daytime, nighttime and low-frequency sound at agreed homes and schools. Record existing traffic and industrial noise. Attach [limits, hours, locations and methods] to the appropriate instrument. Publish commissioning tests and monthly results. For a verified breach, set a deadline for repairs and independent re-testing; refer permit violations to the named authority.
Before generator testing: agree on notice and air monitoring.
Publish the permitted equipment, test schedule, emergency rule and school contacts. Record test and emergency hours separately; independent monitors publish readings and limitations. Residents can report a nuisance without proving a violation. The operator gives a written response within [days]. Air regulators retain enforcement; do not delay an emergency referral for a contract cure period.
At hiring: give nearby residents access to paid work.
A neighborhood-selected workforce partner administers [number of paid places per cohort, wage and budget], including transit or childcare. Publish lawful eligibility and outreach rules and aggregate enrollment by service area. Report completion, placement, wages and retention after six months in aggregate. The independent fund holder verifies fees and wages; missed places trigger a funded second intake. Resident representatives choose any remaining neighborhood grants in public.

Why ask? In January 2026 the New Orleans council reported a temporary hold while planners studied zoning, sound mitigation and a possible one-year interim district. In Lancaster’s agreement, developers must provide a $10 million letter of credit at construction financing. Ask who may draw on security; a credit instrument is not cash received by residents.

Draft 04 · Multi-campus corridor

Count the whole buildout across town lines

Use this alongside any other draft when campuses share a substation, watershed or haul route. Invite neighboring governments, downstream water users, utility planners and residents on both sides of the boundary.

Before the next phase: publish a shared inventory.
Each operator identifies owner, tenant, phase, parcel, expected peak electric demand, peak-day and annual water budget by source, and completion date. An independent regional engineer publishes totals for built, delayed and canceled phases, differentiating measured use from reserved capacity. Adjacent communities review the maps; a planning forum cannot grant permits outside its jurisdiction.
Before connection: allocate common costs once.
Operators and asset owners sign a schedule assigning each road, water and substation cost, payer, share and completion test. Before anyone orders shared works, each operator secures its share of costs that cannot be recovered if it cancels. Name the asset owner who can draw, and credit salvage or reuse. Submit grid cost allocation, collateral and early-exit terms through the serving utility’s applicable process. Publish the non-confidential decision; a company pledge alone does not establish what households will pay.
During operation: share benefits across the boundary.
Each operator pays [amount per operating phase] into an independently administered fund. A board with upstream, downstream and host representatives publishes the allocation formula before the first payment. Audit by operator and phase annually so a shared grant or job is counted once. If a tenant leaves, the named obligor remains liable until agreed replacement security is accepted.

Why ask? Tulsa’s March 2026 permit pause excepted the first two phases of Project Anthem. The Tulsa site record includes the operator’s plans and community responses. List what each phase can already do. Google’s company plan addresses direct grid connection costs; it does not establish how every upstream upgrade will be paid for. For a different kind of commitment, Wisconsin’s commission announced a 15-year minimum term for We Energies’ very large customer rate in April 2026. That local decision does not establish actual household bill impacts.

If your community has a moratorium

Ask the clerk for the adopted text, amendments, effective and end dates, application map, and exemptions for existing approvals. A proposal, an advisory vote and an enacted pause have different effects. A dated tracker entry does not establish that a pause is still in force.

During a pause, use only the activities the text permits. Ask the authorized body to publish outstanding studies, resident objections and what remains unresolved before its next public decision. Compare a smaller project, delay, relocation and no project. A private agreement cannot lift a public restriction.

Find the jurisdiction in the tracker’s Moratoriums directory.

What informed these drafts

The November 11, 2026 tracker snapshot included 357 U.S. moratorium records, 159 policy records, 406 company claims and 232 community responses. The examples above illustrate different decisions; they are not a representative sample or findings about delivery.

  • NAACP: Stop Dirty Data — publish project impacts before bargaining over money; residents need time and independent advice.
  • American Rivers: Data Centers and Rivers — ask separately about water withdrawn, water returned, discharge quality and who pays for new capacity (pp. 7–10).
  • World Resources Institute: Powering Progress — distinguish who signs from who benefits; design training that gives workers options beyond a single campus.
  • Federation of American Scientists: finding eight agreements — ask the clerk for executed contracts and exhibits; a promise to file a later plan leaves terms unsettled.
  • Science for Georgia: community benefit agreements — name the person who measures compliance and the party able to enforce each requirement.

Compare these proposals with Microsoft’s company-wide commitments on power, jobs and taxes. Ask for a local signatory, a figure and a receipt. None of the organizations listed here has endorsed these drafts.
