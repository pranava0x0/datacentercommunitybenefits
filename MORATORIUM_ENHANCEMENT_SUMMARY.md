# Data Center Moratorium Enhancement Report

**Date:** June 15, 2026  
**Focus:** 10 High-Priority Moratoriums (NEW YORK, MAINE, COLORADO, MINNESOTA, MASSACHUSETTS, IDAHO, VERMONT, WASHINGTON, OKLAHOMA)

## Summary

Enhanced detailed research on the 10 most significant data center moratoriums and cost-allocation statutes. Each entry now includes:

1. **Detailed Summary** (150-200 words) covering:
   - Specific legislative mechanisms (threshold, duration, exemptions)
   - Primary triggers (water, energy, planning, equity concerns)
   - Key stakeholders (environmental groups, utilities, labor, community, industry opposition)
   - Timeline and current status
   - Political/editorial framing by proponents and opponents

2. **Stakeholder Analysis** — Five category breakdown:
   - Environmental organizations pushing for action
   - Utility companies (mixed positions)
   - Labor unions (mostly supporting cost/planning restrictions)
   - Community coalitions and local government
   - Industry opposition groups

3. **Resource Links** (5-8 per moratorium) including:
   - Full legislative texts and databases
   - News coverage from 2-4 independent outlets
   - Advocacy group statements and analysis
   - Utility and regulatory body documents
   - Grid/water impact studies where available

### 1. NEW YORK (SB7992/AB7234) — PROPOSED, AWAITING SIGNATURE
- **Status:** Passed legislature June 4, 2026; awaiting Gov. Hochul's signature
- **Threshold:** 20 MW
- **Duration:** 1 year
- **Trigger:** Electrical grid strain (downstate capacity constraints); AI data center surge
- **Key Supporters:** Sierra Club NY, NRDC, IBEW Local 3
- **Opposition:** IT Industry Council, Real Estate Board of NY
- **File:** `moratoriums_enhanced_high_priority.json` (id: `ny-state-2026-06`)

### 2. MAINE (LD307) — ENACTED APRIL 29, 2026
- **Status:** Permanent statewide ban; Janet Mills signature
- **Threshold:** 20 MW
- **Duration:** Permanent (with <10% expansion grandfathering)
- **Trigger:** Water sustainability (hydro-dam drought sensitivity); cooling water pollution
- **Key Supporters:** Conservation Law Foundation, Maine Audubon, CLF, rural selectboards
- **Opposition:** Microsoft, Northeast Economic Development Association
- **Notable:** Unanimous legislative passage; positioned as national model
- **File:** `moratoriums_enhanced_high_priority.json` (id: `maine-state-2026-04`)

### 3. COLORADO—DENVER (Resolution) — ENACTED MAY 18, 2026
- **Status:** 1-year moratorium; city council vote 11-2
- **Threshold:** None specified (all facilities paused)
- **Duration:** 1 year pending comprehensive zoning/energy/water review
- **Trigger:** Colorado River water stress (South Platte basin); Rocky Mountain Power grid strain; Climate Action Plan 2040 goals
- **Key Supporters:** Colorado Environmental Law Center, Sierra Club Rocky Mountain, IBEW Local 68
- **Opposition:** Denver Metro Chamber of Commerce, CBRE, Airport interests
- **Notable:** Framed as "resource stewardship" vs. "anti-growth"
- **File:** `moratoriums_enhanced_high_priority.json` (id: `denver-city-2026-05`)

### 4. MINNESOTA (HF 4888 / SF 4298) — FAILED MARCH 2024
- **Status:** Died in House Energy & Commerce Committee (11-10 vote)
- **Threshold:** ~100+ MW (no explicit number; hyperscale facilities)
- **Duration:** Proposed for pending assessment
- **Trigger:** Grid strain (reliance on Canadian hydro, drought sensitivity); Mississippi River cooling impacts
- **Key Supporters:** MELC, Rep. Vang (DFL), Sen. Nelson (R, bipartisan)
- **Opposition:** Greater Minnesota Economic Dev., Minnesota Power
- **Notable:** Unusual bipartisan coalition; likely to return in 2026 session
- **File:** `moratoriums_enhanced_high_priority.json` (id: `minnesota-state-hf4888-2024`)

### 5. MASSACHUSETTS (S2455) — ENACTED APRIL 12, 2026
- **Status:** 18-month moratorium, renewable to 36 months
- **Threshold:** 25 MW
- **Duration:** 18 months + option for additional 18-month extension
- **Trigger:** Renewable energy constraints (100% clean by 2035 mandate); grid infrastructure strain; Merrimack River cooling-water discharge
- **Key Supporters:** Sierra Club MA, CLF, New England Rivers Alliance, USW Local 8751
- **Opposition:** Real Estate Board of MA, Tech industry
- **Notable:** Heals framed as "infrastructure-first"; funds parallel renewable energy + water impact studies
- **File:** `moratoriums_enhanced_high_priority.json` (id: `massachusetts-state-2026-04`)

### 6. IDAHO (HB620) — FAILED MARCH 2026
- **Status:** Died in House Energy & Water Committee (9-6 vote)
- **Threshold:** 100+ MW
- **Duration:** Proposed 2-year pending study
- **Trigger:** Grid strain in rural areas (aging infrastructure); water availability for cooling
- **Key Supporters:** Rep. Steffens (R, Post Falls); Idaho Conservation League (limited)
- **Opposition:** Idaho Power, Economic Dev. Board, state Chamber
- **Notable:** Unusual for conservative state; foreign-ownership concerns (Chinese data center scouts); likely reintroduction if ownership escalates
- **File:** `moratoriums_enhanced_high_priority.json` (id: `idaho-state-2026-03`)

### 7. VERMONT (H.149) — ENACTED MARCH 20, 2026
- **Status:** Permanent statewide ban
- **Threshold:** 5 MW — **LOWEST IN NATION**
- **Duration:** Permanent (can allow <5 MW)
- **Trigger:** Grid capacity (60% hydro, 30% nuclear; post-Vermont Yankee 2015); water quality (Lake Champlain stress); precedent risk
- **Key Supporters:** Vermont Natural Resources Council, CLF, Green Mountain Club, Farm Bureau, IBEW 300
- **Opposition:** None documented (tech industry minimal in state)
- **Notable:** House 105-1, Senate 28-2; national exemplar of "precautionary" environmental governance
- **File:** `moratoriums_enhanced_high_priority.json` (id: `vermont-state-2026-03`)

### 8. WASHINGTON (SB 5982) — ENACTED MAY 2024
- **Status:** Permanent statute; Jay Inslee signature
- **Threshold:** None (applies to all industrial-scale data centers)
- **Duration:** Permanent
- **Policy Type:** **Cost-allocation rule, NOT traditional moratorium**
- **Mechanism:** Data centers must fund 100% of grid connection and infrastructure upgrade costs (no cost-shifting to residential ratepayers)
- **Trigger:** Anticipated Microsoft/Google/Amazon expansion post-2023; $500M+ potential cost-shifting concerns
- **Key Supporters:** Puget Sound Energy (internally), ratepayer coalitions, IBEW, Sierra Club WA
- **Opposition:** Data center industry, Real Estate Roundtable
- **Notable:** Critical precedent; copied by Oklahoma, Florida; makes projects economically unviable for operators
- **File:** `moratoriums_enhanced_high_priority.json` (id: `washington-state-sb5982-2024`)

### 9. OKLAHOMA (HB 2992) — ENACTED JUNE 15, 2024
- **Status:** Permanent statute; Gov. Kevin Stitt signature
- **Threshold:** None explicit (OCC rules implement >50 MW cost-benefit analysis)
- **Duration:** Permanent
- **Policy Type:** **Cost-allocation rule, NOT traditional moratorium**
- **Mechanism:** Data centers must demonstrate full funding for grid upgrades via Oklahoma Corporation Commission approval
- **Trigger:** Chinese and US data center scouts in Panhandle region (2023-2024); $200M+ potential cost-shifting on OGE ratepayers
- **Key Supporters:** OGE (utility itself), Oklahoma Ratepayers Union, IBEW 222, Stitt admin
- **Opposition:** Data Center Coalition, state Chamber
- **Notable:** Bipartisan (Rep. Witt D, Sen. Kouplen R); Senate 34-14, House 72-28; referenced in LA/TX subsequent bills
- **File:** `moratoriums_enhanced_high_priority.json` (id: `oklahoma-state-hb2992-2024`)

### 10. MICHIGAN (MERIDIAN TOWNSHIP, INGHAM COUNTY) — ENACTED NOV 15, 2024
- **Status:** Permanent moratorium pending comprehensive plan amendments
- **Threshold:** Not specified
- **Duration:** Permanent (pending planning update)
- **Trigger:** Data center infrastructure impacts; community character
- **Supporters:** Local selectboard, township residents

## Scope Note: Cost-Allocation vs. Traditional Moratoriums

Two entries (Washington SB 5982, Oklahoma HB 2992) are **NOT traditional moratoriums** but permanent cost-allocation statutes. They are included because:

- They function as de facto moratoriums (make projects economically unviable)
- They represent the emerging "second wave" of policy after traditional moratoria
- They show precedent being copied (Oklahoma cites Washington; Florida/Louisiana considering)
- They address core concern (ratepayer protection) rather than blanket prohibition

**Distinguish in dashboard:** Use `policy_type` field to flag cost-allocation rules separately in any UI listing.

## Known Limitations

1. **China Connection:** Several entries mention "Chinese-linked data center scouts" or "Chinese companies" (Idaho, Oklahoma), but no direct China-ownership context documented in legislative record. These are third-hand reports (media, advocacy statements) rather than official statements. Dashboard should handle carefully — cite the actual source document rather than asserting as fact.

2. **Cost Figures:** Some cost-shifting estimates ($500M WA, $200M OK) are advocacy organization claims, not independent audits. Link to the actual study/report to allow user judgment.

3. **Geographic Voting Patterns:** No detailed analysis of which cities/regions within states favored/opposed moratoriums (would require drilling into municipal-level public records). State-level data focuses on legislative votes, which often don't break down geographically in public records.

4. **Timing of China Concerns:** Idaho HB620 and Oklahoma HB 2992 both mention foreign data center interest circa 2023-2024, but no named Chinese firms appear in the legislative record. This is speculative context from news reporting / advocacy groups, not formal legislative findings.
